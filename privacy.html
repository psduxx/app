Privacy Policy

DUMC Pty Ltd (ACN [ACN], ABN [ABN]) ("DUMC", "we", "us") Website: https://dumc.com.au

Last updated: 9 November 2026

1. Our approach

This policy explains how we collect, use, disclose and protect personal information. As a small business, DUMC may not be required to comply with the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth). We choose to follow the Australian Privacy Principles (APPs) anyway, and we handle personal information as described here.

2. What we collect

We collect only what we reasonably need to respond to you and run our business. This may include:

  • your name, email address, phone number, job title and organisation;
  • what you tell us in emails, messages and meetings;
  • information about the projects or support you need, including any files you send us;
  • billing details, such as invoicing contacts, addresses and payment records (we do not store card numbers);
  • technical information that third-party services receive when you visit our website (see section 3).

We do not ask for sensitive information, such as health information, and we ask you not to send it. If you send it anyway, we will handle it only as needed to deal with your enquiry.

You may contact us without giving your name (anonymously or under a pseudonym), but we may not be able to help with project or billing matters if you do.

3. Our website

  • Hosting. Our website is a static site hosted on GitHub Pages, which is run by GitHub, Inc. (USA). GitHub may log visitors' IP addresses and technical request data for security and operational purposes. GitHub's own privacy statement applies to that data.
  • No cookies or analytics. We do not currently set cookies or use analytics or advertising trackers. If that changes, we will update this policy first.
  • Fonts. Our pages load fonts from Google Fonts. When they load, your browser connects to Google's servers, which receive your IP address and browser details. Google's privacy policy applies to that data.
  • Contact form. Our contact form does not send or store anything on our website. It opens your own email app with a message addressed to info@dumc.com.au. You can review the message before you send it, and we receive it only if you send it.

4. How we use personal information

We use personal information to:

  • reply to enquiries and prepare quotes and proposals;
  • provide, support and improve our services and software;
  • manage client relationships, invoicing and payments;
  • meet our legal, tax and accounting obligations; and
  • send occasional updates about our services, if you have agreed to receive them or would reasonably expect them. Every marketing email includes an easy way to unsubscribe, as the Spam Act 2003 (Cth) requires.

We do not sell personal information. We do not use it to make decisions that significantly affect you by automated means alone.

5. Who we share it with

We may share personal information with:

  • Email and productivity: our email and documents are hosted on Microsoft 365, with data stored in Australia;
  • Website providers: GitHub and Google, as described in section 3;
  • AI service providers (see section 6);
  • our accountants, bookkeepers, lawyers, insurers and other professional advisers;
  • subcontractors working on your project, who are bound by confidentiality obligations; and
  • government bodies or others where the law requires or allows it.

6. Use of AI services

We may use AI services, such as those provided by Anthropic and OpenAI, to help us process enquiries, draft documents, and develop or support client work. This means information you give us (including personal information in emails or project material) may be processed by those providers.

When we use AI services, we:

  • use business or API accounts whose terms say the provider does not use our inputs to train its models, where such accounts are available;
  • avoid putting in more personal information than the task needs; and
  • follow any instructions in our client agreements about AI use.

If you do not want your information processed by AI services, tell us and we will respect that where we reasonably can.

7. Overseas disclosure

Some providers listed above (including GitHub, Google, Anthropic and OpenAI) may store or process information in the United States and other countries where they or their sub-processors operate. Before we disclose personal information overseas, we take reasonable steps to make sure the recipient handles it consistently with the APPs. This includes choosing reputable providers and relying on their contract terms and security commitments.

8. Security and retention

We protect personal information with reasonable measures, including multi-factor authentication, access controls, encrypted services and keeping devices up to date. No system is completely secure, however.

We keep personal information only as long as we need it for the purposes above or as the law requires (for example, tax records are generally kept for 5 years). After that, we delete it or de-identify it.

If a data breach is likely to cause serious harm, we will notify affected people and, where appropriate, the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (OAIC).

9. Access and correction

You can ask for access to the personal information we hold about you, or ask us to correct it, by emailing info@dumc.com.au. We will respond within 30 days. We may need to verify your identity first. If we refuse a request, we will tell you why (as the law allows) and how to complain. Access is free, although we may charge a reasonable fee for large requests, and we will tell you about any fee in advance.

10. Complaints

If you have a privacy concern, please email info@dumc.com.au with the details. We will acknowledge your complaint within 5 business days and aim to resolve it within 30 days.

If you are not satisfied with our response, you may be able to complain to the OAIC:

  • Website: www.oaic.gov.au
  • Phone: 1300 363 992

11. Changes to this policy

We may update this policy from time to time. The current version will always be on our website, with the date it was last updated.
